Most folks before encountering Medplum think of FHIR in one of two ways:

- As a read-only facade for traditional, proprietary EHRs: Epic, DaVinci Payer Exchange, etc all use FHIR only at the boundary, for interfacing.

- As an OLAP backend landing area, alongside a workflow-serving datastore: HAPI, Aidbox, HealthLake, Azure/Google FHIR stores, published for data
  exchange, analytics, or compliance

As such, when devs come to Medplum, they usually ask a question along the following lines:

- "How is the data is handled and stored in the DB itself? How does this relate to FHIR?"
- "If I point a SQL Explorer at the Postgres database when running Medplum locally, will I be able to see what is happening?"
- "Can I get direct access to the rows in the database when using your hosted service?"

These are all very reasonable questions for developers doing due diligence on Medplum, and part of the beauty of open source is that developers
can inspect our implementation directly! We also think that these questions come from a different understanding of the relationship between FHIR
and an EHR, and we hope to explain Medplum's relationship with FHIR in this blog.
